An independent strategic review for a major UK telecoms operator

Twelve weeks, three phases, and a brief from the sponsor that set the standard from day one: not another strategy review that sits on a shelf, but specific actions with owners and dates.

Independent review Summer and autumn 2026 Delivered by Stratus Systems Ltd
The Engagement

What Stratus Systems was asked to do

Stratus Systems Ltd was engaged by a major UK telecoms operator to carry out an independent strategic review of one of its business lines. It was a fixed-fee engagement in three phases - Discovery, Analysis and Synthesis - designed so that the output would reach the senior leadership team in time to inform their planning cycle.

The review was asked to answer a set of broad questions rather than one narrow one:

  • Is the business line worth fixing and investing in, or should it be divested or handed to a specialist?
  • Where is the market going, and where should limited investment be focused?
  • What does the technology platform decision need to be?
  • What is wrong with the go-to-market and sales model, and what would fix it?
  • How should the organisation be shaped so that it can actually deliver?
  • What is the investment case, and what is the plan to implement it?
Approach

How the work was done

1

Discovery

Around 65 one-to-one conversations across product, sales, finance, technology, networks, strategy and partner teams, alongside customer and supplier perspectives and external market evidence.

2

Analysis

Market forecasts, internal finance data, public-source research on competitors and technologies, and audits of customer-facing digital channels. Where internal numbers disagreed, the conflict was flagged and resolved rather than one side being picked.

3

Synthesis

A single recommended direction rather than a menu of options, with an implementation plan and, for every unresolved point, a proposed way to close it and a proposed owner.

Workshops that tested alignment

Five cross-functional workshops deliberately put people with opposing views, and owners of overlapping initiatives, in the same room. That tested real alignment rather than assuming it, and views were never attributed to individuals.

No big reveal at the end

Monthly executive touchpoints meant leadership saw hypotheses first, then proof, then final recommendations. Nothing in the final paper should have been a surprise.

Deliverables

What was handed over

  • A working paper at the end of the analysis phase, followed by a final paper with an executive summary, analytical sections, an implementation section and a closing list of open items.
  • A leadership presentation, refined with the client's own team so that every figure in the paper matched the deck.
  • Supporting investment material, with the headline figure built only from sourced evidence and tested against challenge.
  • Facilitation packs, call-preparation briefs and open-source research assessments along the way.
What Came Out of It

Outcomes

A decision, not a debate

The central question of whether to fix and invest or to divest was resolved part-way through the engagement with a clear recommendation, rather than being left open until the end.

Numbers that agree

The review surfaced and corrected inconsistent figures in the client's own reporting before they reached a governance process.

A structural diagnosis

Under-delivery had a structural cause, not a personal one: ownership was fragmented across several teams and incentives did not reward the right behaviour. The recommended fix was a single accountable unit.

Action during the work

Several fixes and decisions were triggered while the engagement was still running, tracked in a standing log, rather than waiting for the final paper.

The recommendation was built so that the people who must deliver it were aligned behind it. The aim was a horse, not a camel designed by committee.

  • Interviewing that draws out people's own views before findings are revealed, asks peers the same question independently, and separates what people said from what was inferred.
  • Facilitating sensitive, cross-functional sessions where views are opposed.

  • Turning analysis into a single decisive recommendation plus an owned, dated plan to close the gaps.
